# Usability test protocol (optional; 07 §10)

Owner: C (runs it), A (observes the science answers). Three students from outside the team, no coaching. The test checks the learning goal: after two minutes, a student can say how fast rain changed in each of two regions, whether the two differ, and that "can't tell yet" is not "no change".

**Pass:** at least two of the three students manage each of the four tasks (U, `docs/07_TESTS.md` §10).

## Rules

- **Consent first:** read the consent line aloud; anyone may stop at any time.
- **Anonymous:** record initials and a role ("student, 16") only. **No photos, video or audio of anyone under 18**, and no recognisable names, voices or faces in any video or on the project page (Space Apps rule).
- **No coaching:** do not explain, hint or point. If someone is stuck for 60 seconds, mark the task "not managed" and move on.
- Use the real build (Part 5 onwards) on a laptop and on a phone, in the language the student prefers.

Consent line: "We are testing our website, not you. There are no wrong answers. It takes about 10 minutes, we don't record your face or voice, and you can stop at any time. Is that OK?"

## Tasks

| # | Say it like this | Managed means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "You want to know whether rain changed differently in <A> and <B>. Find the data you would use." | Picks a question card and a suitable variable (rain), with no help |
| 2 | "How fast did rain change in <A>? Tell me in your own words." | States a rate of change with its unit and time scale ("about 30 mm per decade") |
| 3 | "What does this range mean?" (point at the 95% range) | Explains it as a range of rates that fit the data, without saying "95% chance it's true" |
| 4 | Open a result that says "Can't tell yet". "What does this mean?" | Reads it as "the record can't decide", not as "no change" |

Then ask: "Did rain change differently in the two places?" (note the answer; it shows whether the trend in the difference came across) and "What confused you most?"

## If a task fails

Fix the screen or the wording, then test that task again with students who have not seen the site. Record both rounds.
